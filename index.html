How to Price a $30 Handmade Item After Etsy Fees

A $30 price tag feels fair for a handmade item. It covers materials, it respects your time, and it looks reasonable to a buyer. But the price a buyer pays is not the money you keep. Etsy takes four separate bites out of every order, and on a $30 item they add up to more than most new sellers expect.

Here is the maths for a $30 order in the United States. The listing fee is $0.20. The transaction fee is 6.5% of the price plus shipping, which is $1.95. Payment processing is 3% plus $0.25, which is $1.15. Add them together and the fees come to $3.30. You keep $26.70 before materials, packaging, postage, and your own time. That is 11% of the sale price gone on fees alone.

Now work backwards. Suppose your materials cost $8, packaging costs $1, and postage costs $4. Your real costs are $13 plus $3.30 in fees, or $16.30 in total, leaving $13.70 for your time. If the item takes an hour to make, you are earning $13.70 an hour. If it takes two hours, you are earning $6.85 an hour, which is below minimum wage in most US states. The $30 price that felt fair is suddenly a bad deal.

This is why a minimum viable price matters more than a round number. Your minimum viable price is the lowest price at which the item still pays for itself after fees and costs. For the item above, with one hour of work valued at $20, the maths is $8 materials plus $1 packaging plus $4 postage plus $20 time, which is $33 before fees. Because fees take about 11%, the price needs to be around $37 to leave $33 after Etsy takes its cut. The item should be listed at $37, not $30.

Three habits protect your margin. First, price from your costs upward, never from a competitor's price downward. Second, remember the transaction fee applies to shipping too, so "free shipping" means you absorb both the postage and the fee on it. Third, check the crossover point where your own store becomes cheaper. Shopify's Basic plan is $39 a month with 2.9% plus $0.30 processing, so on a $30 order your own store costs about $1.17 against Etsy's $3.30. The gap is $2.13 per order, and the plan pays for itself at around nineteen orders a month.
